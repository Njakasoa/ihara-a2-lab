# Independent finite certificate for the Duursma–Mak genus-343 model

This is a finite-field audit of the data used in Duursma–Mak, §5.3, pp. 18–19.
The paper is the published source for the construction; the residue matrices and
place-trace table below are independently recomputed.  The machine-readable
local residue output is in `certificates/dm-finite-geometry.json` and the
canonical low-degree place representatives are in
`certificates/dm-selected-places.json` and `curves/k343-places.json`.

## Normalized equations

Work over `F_2` and write

\[
a=x^3+x+1,\qquad b=x^2+x,\qquad c=x^5+x^2+1,
\]

with

\[
H=F_2(x,y),\qquad y^2+a y=b.
\]

For the five-dimensional `F_2`-space

\[
V=\langle 1,x,x^2,y,y^2\rangle_{F_2},
\]

the displayed equation in the paper is

\[
v_h^2+c v_h=b h \qquad (h\in V).
\]

After `w_h=v_h/c`, this is the Artin–Schreier equation

\[
w_h^2+w_h=f_h,qquad f_h=bh/c^2.
\]

The paper’s p. 18 phrase “extensions `E(v)`” is a symbol error: the preceding
paragraph has changed the base to `H`, and every coefficient above belongs to
`H`.  The field generated by a basis of the `w_h` is therefore denoted `k/H`.

## The base curve

The cubic `a` is irreducible over `F_2`.  In the quadratic presentation of `H`,
the three roots of `a` in `F_8` give one ramified degree-three place.  Reducing
`b/a^2` at each root gives a nonzero simple Artin–Schreier residue, so the
quadratic extension has different exponent two there and nowhere else.  Thus
Riemann–Hurwitz gives `2g(H)-2=-4+2*3=2`, hence `g(H)=2`.

At infinity, with `t=1/x` and `Y=t^3y`, the equation becomes

\[
Y^2+(1+t^2+t^3)Y=t^4(1+t).
\]

The two values `Y=0,1` at `t=0` give two rational places.  Direct enumeration
of the normalized curve gives `a_1(H)=6` and, through degree ten,

\[
(a_d(H))_{d=1}^{10}=(6,0,1,1,6,13,12,33,56,105).
\]

As a cross-check, the first four counts give
`P_H(T)=1+3T+5T^2+6T^3+4T^4`; Newton recurrence predicts the displayed
counts through degree ten.

## The two degree-five places and conductor 2

The polynomial `c` has no root in `F_2` and is not divisible by the sole
irreducible quadratic `x^2+x+1`, hence it is irreducible of degree five.  Put
`F_32=F_2[alpha]` with `alpha^5=alpha^2+1` and use the polynomial basis
`1,alpha,...,alpha^4`, encoded by the binary integer whose bit `i` is the
coefficient of `alpha^i`.  At `x=alpha`, the two solutions of the `H` equation
are

\[
y_1=17=\alpha^4+1,
\qquad y_2=26=\alpha^4+\alpha^3+\alpha.
\]

The ten conjugate geometric points form two Frobenius orbits, hence two degree-
five places `P_5,P'_5` of `H`.

For a root of `c`, take `t=x-alpha` as local parameter.  Since `c'=x^4` in
characteristic two and the square of a power series has no odd-degree term,

\[
\frac{N}{c^2}=\frac{N(alpha)}{c'(alpha)^2t^2}
 +\frac{N'(alpha)}{c'(alpha)^2t}+O(1).
\]

Subtracting `u^2+u` with
`u=sqrt(N(alpha)/c'(alpha)^2)/t` leaves the reduced residue

\[
rho(h)=\frac{(bh)'(alpha)}{c'(alpha)^2}
 +\sqrt{\frac{b(alpha)h(alpha)}{c'(alpha)^2}}.
\]

Here

\[
y'=\frac{1+(x^2+1)y}{x^3+x+1},
\qquad (1,x,x^2,y,y^2)'=(0,1,0,y',0).
\]

For the representative with `y=17`, the five residues for
`(1,x,x^2,y,y^2)` are

\[
(23,19,12,30,15),
\]

and for `y=26` they are

\[
(23,19,12,5,3).
\]

The entries use the polynomial-basis encoding above.  The two resulting
`5 x 5` matrices over `F_2` both have determinant one (row reduction gives the
five standard basis vectors), so each local map `V -> F_32` has rank five.
The Frobenius-conjugate points have the same rank; all ten points are also
listed in `certificates/dm-finite-geometry.json`.

Consequences:

1. Every nonzero `h` has a reduced simple pole at both `P_5` and `P'_5`, so
   its Artin conductor is exactly `2P_5+2P'_5`, of degree twenty.
2. No nonzero linear combination of the five Artin–Schreier classes is trivial.
   Thus `Gal(k/H)=(Z/2Z)^5` and `[k:H]=32`.
3. The complete local inertia at each of the two degree-five places has order
   32, so each place is totally ramified with residue degree one.

The last two statements concern the explicit finite cover `k/H`.  They do not
compute the order or structure of the later ray-field group `Gal(K/H)` in
Theorem 1.1.

## Rational splitting and genus

At the four affine rational points (`x=0,1`), `b=0` and `c=1`, so every
`f_h` is zero.  At the two infinity branches, `x` has pole order one, `c` pole
order five, and `y` has pole order at most three; hence `bh/c^2` vanishes at
both places.  The six rational places of `H` therefore split completely in
`k`, giving `a_1(k)=6*32=192`.

There are 31 nontrivial characters of `Gal(k/H)`, each of conductor degree 20.
The conductor–discriminant formula gives

\[
2g(k)-2=32(2g(H)-2)+31*20=32*2+620=684,
\]

so `g(k)=343`.

## Independent place-trace table

For an unramified place `Q` of `H`, define

\[
tau(Q)=\bigl(Tr_{kappa(Q)/F_2}(f_1(Q)),\ldots,
Tr_{kappa(Q)/F_2}(f_5(Q))\bigr)\in F_2^5.
\]

The five entries use the basis `(1,x,x^2,y,y^2)`.  If `tau(Q)=0`, `Q` splits
into 32 places of the same degree in `k`; if `tau(Q) != 0`, its Frobenius has
order two, giving 16 places of twice the degree.  The table is from direct
enumeration of normalized `H` points and Frobenius orbits; “ramified” means
`c=0`.

| degree `d` | `a_d(H)` | unramified, `tau=0` | unramified, `tau!=0` | ramified `c=0` |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 6 | 6 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 4 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 5 | 6 | 0 | 4 | 2 |
| 6 | 13 | 0 | 13 | 0 |
| 7 | 12 | 0 | 12 | 0 |
| 8 | 33 | 0 | 33 | 0 |
| 9 | 56 | 0 | 56 | 0 |
| 10 | 105 | 0 | 105 | 0 |

The degree-three, degree-four and four unramified degree-five rows account for
the first new places in `k`: respectively 16 places of degrees 6 and 8, and 64
places of degree 10.  The two ramified degree-five places remain one place each.
No other row through degree ten contributes a place of degree at most ten.  Thus

\[
(a_d(k))_{d=1}^{10}=(192,0,0,0,2,16,0,16,0,64),
\]

matching the paper.  These are exact finite counts; they are not, by themselves,
a proof of an infinite class-field tower and they do not replace the later
ray-field character calculation.
